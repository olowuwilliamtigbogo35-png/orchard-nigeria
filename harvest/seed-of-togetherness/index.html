Literary Meditation

One Cloth, Many Hands

A Literary Meditation Inspired by the Unity Fabric Competition✥

The Planting

✥

The Seed of Togetherness was planted in the field of national unity, cultural identity and creative expression by Senator Oluremi Tinubu through the Unity Fabric Competition. The initiative invited Nigerians to weave their diversity into a shared national symbol, affirming that the nation's greatest strength lies not in uniformity but in the harmonious blending of its many cultures, traditions and people.

The Story

✥

There was once an old weaver whose workshop stood at the end of a narrow street.

People travelled from distant places to admire the beautiful fabrics that emerged from her loom, yet few understood how they came to life.

One morning, a group of children gathered around her table.

Before them lay threads of every colour, some bright, some faded, some frayed enough to look too broken to use.

Green.
Blue.
Gold.
White.
Brown.
Red.

A little boy frowned.

“Why do you use so many colours?” he asked. “Would it not be easier to weave with just one?”

The old woman smiled but did not answer.

Instead, she picked up a single thread and held it before them.

“What can this become on its own?”

“A thread,” the children replied.

She nodded, then picked up another, and another. Slowly, she placed them across the loom.

The green crossed the gold.

The white embraced the blue.

The red met the brown.

The children watched as her hands moved with quiet confidence.

At first, they saw only threads.

Then, little by little, something remarkable began to appear.

The threads did not lose their colours.

None tried to become another.

Yet together they formed a fabric far more beautiful than any single thread could ever have become.

The children stood in silence, watching the pattern emerge until no one thought to ask another question.

Years later, one of those children became a celebrated textile designer.

Whenever people praised the beauty of his creations, he always remembered the old weaver.

“They think beauty comes from colours,” he would say.

“But beauty comes from knowing how different colours belong together.”

For he had learnt that the strongest fabric is never woven from one thread alone.

Neither is the strongest nation.

The Vision Behind the Seed

✥

Unity is not the absence of differences.

It is the celebration of them.

Every culture carries beauty.

Every tradition carries wisdom.

Every community contributes a unique thread to the fabric of the nation.

When diversity is embraced with mutual respect, it becomes a source of strength rather than division.

A united nation is not one where everyone looks the same.

It is one where everyone belongs.

For the strongest fabric is woven from many threads.

The Harvest

✥

The harvest of this seed includes:

  • Greater appreciation of Nigeria's cultural diversity.
  • Promotion of national unity through creative expression.
  • Increased public participation in celebrating a shared national identity.
  • Recognition of fashion, textiles, and art as instruments of nation building.
  • Stronger intercultural understanding among Nigerians.
  • Renewed conviction that unity is strengthened when diversity is honoured.

Legacy Reflection

✥

No fabric is woven from a single thread.

Its beauty emerges as many colours, patterns, and textures find their place beside one another.

So it is with a nation.

Its strength is not found in the sameness of its people, but in their willingness to weave their differences into a shared future.

Every culture enriches the design.

Every tradition adds another strand of meaning.

Every community strengthens the whole.

For when every thread is valued, the fabric endures.
