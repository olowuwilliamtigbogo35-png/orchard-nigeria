A True Story

The Girl Who Came Back

Ebere Ndulue was fifteen when life suddenly became heavier than a child should have to carry.

✥

Her mother was frail and unwell, and as the only daughter in the home, Ebere felt responsible for holding the family together. Then a young man entered her life. He promised to help her care for her mother and support the family. She believed him.

Instead, she became pregnant.

It was not the future she had imagined. She spoke of embarrassment and shame. Her mother was devastated too. The daughter in whom she had placed so much hope was now facing motherhood while still a child herself.

For many girls, that moment becomes the end of school. It becomes the point at which people begin to speak about them in the past tense: what they could have been; what they should have done; the life they have lost.

But Ebere’s story did not end there.

The Return

✥

She found a place at the Alternative High School for Girls in Agboju, Lagos: a school created for girls whose education had been interrupted by early pregnancy, poverty and other difficult circumstances.

There, she was not treated as a failure. She was given the chance to learn again, to acquire vocational skills and to see herself as more than the hardest chapter of her young life.

Her mother stood with her. The school stood with her. And, slowly, a future that had seemed closed began to open again.

Why It Matters

✥

Ebere Ndulue’s life reminds us why education for girls must never depend on a perfect past. A girl may have made a painful decision, endured hardship or become a mother too early. Yet she still has a mind to develop, a gift to discover and a future worth protecting.

The Alternative High School for Girls is not simply a building. For girls like Ebere, it is a return: to learning, to dignity and to the right to dream again.
